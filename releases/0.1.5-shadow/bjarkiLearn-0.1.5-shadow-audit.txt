REVIEW CONCLUSIONS
1. Correction authority remained intact. Reassignment checks both the old and new actor/time/family overlap. Retraction and changed evidence still withdraw affected serving generations, including when a replay row is already retired or another positive keeps the current label unchanged. Obsolete or duplicate revisions do not gain fresh authority.
2. Forecast continuity remained intact. Routine occurrence/example retirement can censor retained replay data and rebuild a candidate while preserving a completed serving snapshot. Genuine contradictory evidence still invalidates the affected model. Lead-only updates alone do not spuriously revise training labels or discard an unaffected partial candidate.
3. Future evidence remained temporally contained. Features and witnesses project at the requested time; query calls do not mature outcomes. Accepted observed time batches eligible positive maturity, reconciles retained rows, and checks serving guards. Rollback/reset preserves observed positives while censoring unobserved future outcomes.
4. Bounds remained intact: 16 actors, 128 retained occurrences, 256 retained examples, at most 256 serving correction descriptors, and at most 256 candidate replay rows. Mixed capacity traces and full-corpus replacement exercised retirement, stale replay, actor recreation and monotone retention-floor protections.
5. Reset/reload and copied projections remained consistent. Deterministic sealing and replay matched 0.1.4. Malformed restore inputs left active state intact; valid replacement detached caller data. Public reads neither mutated evidence nor performed optimizer work. Native optimizer work remained deferred during known or unreadable combat.

INTERPRETATION AND REMAINING LIMITS
No new issue is assigned a severity or classified as a regression. Agreement with 0.1.4 alone would not rule out a shared defect; this audit also used focused contract assertions, exact numeric oracles and separately rebuilt clean models. All tests remain finite.

Documented bounded-history behavior is unchanged: discarded identities cannot be reconstructed indefinitely; sealed imported examples intentionally lack raw correction identifiers; an old serving snapshot can retain retired training influence until a complete replacement is ready. A retired far-future identity can conservatively advance the retention floor. These are explicit limitations, not newly confirmed bugs.

The optional SharedEvidence bus has no autonomous delayed replay timer for a row rejected as future-dated at subscription. A later qualifying replay/publication is needed. If enable occurs with an unreadable clock, the optional bridge retries on a later addon-load event rather than an autonomous retry. These existing boundaries are recorded in the native report; no new regression was demonstrated.

Still unverified: actual client API behavior and event payloads, native secret-value enforcement, taint, live scheduling/frame-time cost, practical predictive usefulness, probability calibration and consumer benefit. No performance improvement or live correctness claim follows from this audit.
